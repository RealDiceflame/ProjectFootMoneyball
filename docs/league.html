What this model does—and doesn't—know

Each game uses the same paired historical forecast-error model as the head-to-head simulator. One shared score settles each matchup, so opponents cannot both win. Current team strengths are held fixed through the remaining season; games are independent conditional on those strengths. Injuries, starters, roster changes, coaching, weather and season-long changes in strength are not simulated.

Only completed regular-season scores in the saved feed are fixed. A game underway or awaiting a final result is simulated as unresolved, without live scores or in-game adjustments. The feed uses a conservative six-hour completion delay. Actual playoff results are not ingested yet: the postseason shown here is hypothetical.

Seeding uses overall winning percentage (a tie is half a win), head-to-head, division/conference/common-game results where applicable, strength of victory and schedule. Late tiebreakers are simplified to overall point differential and a seeded draw; official combined scoring ranks and net touchdowns are not reproduced. Therefore close seeds can differ from official NFL tiebreakers. Division winners occupy seeds 1–4; wild cards 5–7. No. 1 gets a bye, the divisional round reseeds, higher seeds host within the conference, and the Super Bowl is neutral.

Postseason sampling conditions on a decisive score: no playoff game ends tied. It is not a drive-by-drive or overtime-rule simulation. Mean ± SD and percentile ranges describe sampled variation, not calibrated confidence intervals.
